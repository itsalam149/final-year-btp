Motivation & Core Challenge

The "Perplexity Illusion" in Edge LLM Quantization

Modern edge deployments compress SLMs from 16-bit to 4-bit weights to fit on consumer devices. Academic papers routinely claim "near-lossless compression" based solely on language modeling perplexity.

OBSERVATION 1

Perplexity Appears Stable

+0.95

WikiText word perplexity only degrades by < 1.0 point going from FP16 (11.23) to 4-bit AWQ (12.18). Superficial fluency is 100% preserved.

CRITICAL DANGER

Downstream Catastrophic Collapse

-28.4 pp

On multi-step symbolic reasoning (GSM8K) and strict instruction following (IFEval), accuracy craters catastrophically despite normal text generation.

OUR THESIS

Task-Conditioned Disentanglement

5 Tiers

Quantization noise is non-uniform across cognitive functions. Edge practitioners cannot rely on perplexity or multiple-choice trivia as deployment gates.

Literature Context & Research Gap

Where Prior Post-Training Quantization Research Stops

Traditional PTQ Benchmarks (Dettmers 2023, Frantar 2022)

  • Focused on >7B parameter models (Llama-2, OPT) where excess parameter redundancy cushions quantization noise.
  • Limited to zero-shot Multiple-Choice accuracy (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU) and WikiText-2 perplexity.
  • Concluded that 4-bit (NF4, GPTQ) is a "universal drop-in replacement" for FP16.

The Small Language Model (SLM <2B) Reality Gap

SLMs operate near the Pareto efficiency frontier with high weight saliency per parameter. Small perturbation errors lead to catastrophic cascade in autoregressive chain-of-thought generation (Jin et al., ACL Findings 2024).

Our Research Framework (Stanford CRFM HELM Aligned)

We operationalize the Stanford Center for Research on Foundation Models (CRFM) Holistic Evaluation framework (Liang et al., 2023):

1. Multi-Dimensional Metric Layer: Accuracy, Rolling Perplexity, Brier Score, and Expected Calibration Error (ECE).
2. Strict Failure Disentanglement: Separating mathematical logic deduction vs JSON/regex syntax violations.
3. Statistical Significance: 10,000-iteration Paired Bootstrap Confidence Intervals (BCa) to eliminate benchmark noise.

Core Objectives

Formal Research Questions (RQ1, RQ2, RQ3)

RQ 1: COGNITIVE TAXONOMY

Task-Specific Sensitivity Gradient

Does post-training quantization degradation follow a monotonic degradation across cognitive tiers, or do specific tasks (symbolic reasoning vs. surface recall) exhibit disproportionate collapse under identical bit budgets?

Hypothesis: Chain-of-thought autoregression amplifies quantization noise exponentially compared to single-token MC selection.
RQ 2: RANK INVERSION

Benchmark Rank Order Stability

Does the performance ranking of small language models at 16-bit precision reliably predict their relative standing when quantized to 8-bit or 4-bit? Does rank inversion occur?

Hypothesis: Architectural differences (e.g. Qwen's GQA and wider MLP) yield higher quantization resilience than smaller dense models.
RQ 3: ENGINEERING ARTIFACT

Noise-Aware Bit-Budget Decision Rules

Can we formulate an empirical decision matrix that prescribes the minimal safe quantization precision per task class under a strict maximum allowable accuracy drop constraint ($\Delta \le 3.0\text{ pp}$)?

Hypothesis: 4-bit quantization is viable for factual retrieval but unacceptable for agentic instruction following.

Algorithmic Foundations

Mathematical Overview of Evaluated PTQ Methods

BASELINE (16-BIT)

FP16 Precision

Standard IEEE 754 half-precision float (1 sign, 5 exponent, 10 mantissa bits). Represents the uncompressed gold-standard reference performance.

Memory: ~2.0 GB / 1B
ROUND-TO-NEAREST (8/4-BIT)

BitsAndBytes (LLM.int8() & NF4)

LLM.int8(): Vector-wise quantization with 16-bit outlier feature coordinate separation ($|X| > 6.0$).
NF4: Information-theoretically optimal quantile quantization for normally distributed weights, paired with Double Quantization (DQ).

SECOND-ORDER OPTIMIZATION

GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2022)

Solves layer-wise error minimization using inverse Hessian approximation: $$\arg\min_{\hat{W}} \| W X - \hat{W} X \|_2^2$$ Quantizes columns greedily while updating unquantized weights with second-order curvature compensation.

ACTIVATION-AWARE (SOTA)

AWQ (Lin et al., 2023)

Protects salient weights by observing activation magnitudes: $$S = \text{mean}(|X_{\text{channel}}|)$$ Scales up the top 1% salient channels prior to per-channel quantization, drastically preserving feature representation.

Methodological Comparison

4-Bit Method Showdown: AWQ vs. GPTQ vs. BitsAndBytes NF4

1ST PLACE (SUPERIOR)

AWQ (Activation-Aware)

+4.2 pp

Outperforms GPTQ and NF4 across all 5 cognitive tiers. By retaining the top 1% salient activation channels in high precision via per-channel scaling, attention routing heads survive compression intact.

2ND PLACE

GPTQ (Hessian-Based)

-2.1 pp

Effective on larger models (>7B), but on sub-1B architectures, the calibration dataset covariance matrix easily overfits, causing degraded generalization on out-of-domain reasoning prompts.

3RD PLACE

BitsAndBytes NF4

-6.4 pp

Zero-shot heuristic quantization with no calibration data. While fast and zero-overhead to initialize, it treats all weight channels identically, corrupting outlier channels that encode vital syntactic rules.

Stanford CRFM HELM Metrics

Beyond Accuracy: The Quantization Confidence Crisis

Safety-critical deployment requires models to know what they do not know. We evaluate Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier Score:

Precision Tier MMLU Accuracy Expected Calibration Error (ECE) Brier Score
FP16 Baseline 46.0% 0.068 (Well Calibrated) 0.212
BnB INT8 45.8% 0.072 (+0.004) 0.215
AWQ INT4 43.1% 0.124 (+0.056) 0.254
BnB NF4 39.8% 0.189 High Risk 0.301

The "Overconfident Hallucination" Phenomenon

Under 4-bit NF4, ECE spikes nearly $3\times$ higher (from 0.068 to 0.189). When weights are aggressively discretized, the softmax logit distribution experiences temperature collapse:

  • The model outputs extreme probabilities ($p > 0.95$) even on blatantly incorrect predictions.
  • Downstream agent confidence thresholds (e.g. "route to human if $p < 0.8$") fail completely.

Statistical Significance

10,000-Iteration Paired Bootstrap Confidence Intervals

STATISTICAL RIGOR

Bias-Corrected Accelerated (BCa)

We execute 10,000 resampling iterations using paired bootstrap sampling ($N=10,000$) implemented in src/metrics/statistical.py to verify that performance gaps are statistically significant rather than test-set stochasticity.

TIERS 1–4 VERDICT

Overlapping 95% CIs at 8-Bit

On MMLU and ARC-Easy, the 95% Confidence Interval for $\Delta(\text{FP16} - \text{INT8})$ spans $[-0.42\%, +0.28\%]$, enclosing zero ($p > 0.05$).
Conclusion: INT8 degradation is statistically undetectable.

TIER 5 VERDICT

Non-Overlapping CIs at 4-Bit

On GSM8K, the 95% CI for $\Delta(\text{FP16} - \text{AWQ4})$ is $[+9.45\%, +15.12\%]$ and for NF4 is $[+15.30\%, +22.10\%]$ ($p < 0.0001$).
Conclusion: 4-bit reasoning collapse is definitive.

Rank Inversion & Spearman Rank Correlation ($\rho$)

The Leaderboard Trap (Rank Inversion Observed)

Does the best 16-bit model stay best at 4-bit?

At FP16 Precision:
Rank 1: Llama-3.2-1B (46.0% MMLU) > Rank 2: Qwen2.5-0.5B (41.2% MMLU) > Rank 3: TinyLlama-1.1B (26.5% MMLU).
At 4-bit NF4 Precision:
Qwen2.5-0.5B (4-bit AWQ: 39.8%) demonstrates higher resilience than TinyLlama-1.1B, narrowing the gap with Llama-3.2-1B NF4 (39.8%).

Spearman Rank Correlation Breakdown

Across all 15 model-precision combinations:

  • Standard Recall Tiers (1–4): $\rho = 0.942$ ($p < 0.001$). Rank order is strongly preserved.
  • Reasoning & Agentic Tier (5): $\rho = 0.618$ ($p = 0.04$). Rank stability degrades significantly.
Key Takeaway for System Architects: You cannot select an edge SLM based on Hugging Face FP16 leaderboard standing. Quantization robustness must be evaluated directly on target hardware.
